What you get

01 / 05

Live charts

01

Line or candlesticks over six ranges, with a price scale and a time axis. Drag it any way — sideways through time, up and down through prices — scroll or pinch to zoom where you point, and finer candles load as you go in. A log axis, an average that says what it covers, US inflation releases marked where they fall, and every point readable from the keyboard.

Draw and measure

02

Lines, trend lines, rays, boxes and notes that stay with the coin at their price and time. A ruler that says the change, the time and how often a move that far happened in that long. A line can become a price target.

Counted studies

03

Volume by price, where the price sits in each range, the usual range ahead as a cone, regimes and the levels where it has turned. Each was tested on years of history first, and each says what it counts — never what comes next.

Compare with anything

04

Another coin, the S&P 500, the Nasdaq 100 or gold, from a drawer beside the chart. Both drawn as percent change from the same moment on one shared axis, with the gap, how often they stepped the same way and how closely they moved — so where the lines cross is where the market put them.

Chart companion

05

Sixteen chart patterns — head and shoulders, triangles, wedges, ranges, flags — twelve strategy setups and twenty-one readings such as divergences, the Ichimoku cloud and Fibonacci retracements, named where they appear. Each sits beside what followed it on this coin and how it did when tested on four coins. Counted, never scored.

Call the next move

06

The right of the chart becomes a board of squares — real price bands at real moments ahead, each with its chance, worked out from the coin's own history and checked against years of past squares before it was allowed on. Drag the board to look further, click a square twice, and it settles itself: called it, or missed. The score is local, worth nothing, and never leaves.

Price targets

07

"BTC rises above 80,000", or "BTC falls 5% in 1h, 4h or 24h". Reported next time you open a tab — even a move that happened and reverted overnight — and, only if you switch it on, as a Chrome notification.

TARGET

Portfolio

08

Holdings by amount, or a public address read for its balance. Dated purchases, sales and income; cost basis by FIFO, LIFO or HIFO; the return month by month. Press H and the amounts are masked.

Crypto tax guide

09

The crypto tax rules of 244 countries and territories — what is taxed and what is not, the rates, the tax year — each read from the country's own tax authority or law, linked and dated. Where none was confirmed, it says so. Not tax advice.

Practice derivatives

10

A perpetual-contract terminal priced from a real venue's public quotes: long or short up to 200x, stops, limit orders, funding and liquidation. Settled in Practice Units that cannot be bought or cashed out — no real money, ever.

News, dated and read

11

Fourteen sources, filtered to your coins or your holdings, with advertising kept out and one story written up four times shown once. Click a headline and the story opens beside the list without its pictures, with a reading of it: what kind of story its words make it, the coins and figures it names, and what the chart did since. Counted, never judged.

Has this happened before?

12

What followed every past time this coin entered the state it is in now — with the count behind every figure, and no comparison at all below twelve episodes. It opens on what came after everything true right now, and lists the twenty past stretches most like the last month with what followed each. What got built instead of buy and sell signals.

Private by design

03 / 05
0Permissions at install
0Accounts
0Trackers

PriceTab asks for no browser permission at install. No account, no analytics, no telemetry. Your coins, settings, portfolio, targets and practice account live in your browser's local storage and never leave the device — which is also why they don't follow you to another computer, unless you carry a backup file there yourself.

Prices come from public, keyless market APIs. The one thing that leaves the browser beyond a request for market data is a wallet address, and only if you paste one in to watch its balance — then it goes to that chain's balance provider and nowhere else. Checking it against the sanctions list happens on your device, from a list that ships with the extension. Two things are optional and off until you press a button inside the app: a Chrome notification for a hit target, and eight newsroom feeds.

It also means a target can only be checked while a PriceTab tab is open. Nothing runs in the background when your browser is closed: there is no background worker to run it.

Verify it yourself

04 / 05

A browser extension can read every page you open, so being suspicious of one is the correct instinct — including of this page, which is marketing. Below is the part that isn't: the manifest Chrome enforces, every host the code can reach, and three checks that take a minute each.

manifest.json — every key, long values shortened at …
{
  "manifest_version": 3,
  "name": "PriceTab - Crypto Charts on Every New Tab",
  "version": "1.5.0",
  "default_locale": "en",
  "description": "Every new tab opens a live crypto chart…",
  "chrome_url_overrides": { "newtab": "index.html" },
  "permissions": [],
  "optional_permissions": ["notifications"],
  "optional_host_permissions": ["https://cointelegraph.com/*", … eight newsroom feeds],
  "action": { "default_popup": "popup.html" },
  "icons": { "16": …, "48": …, "128": … }
}

// Eleven keys. "permissions" is empty, and the two optional lists
// are granted only when you press a button inside the app. There is no
// "host_permissions", no "background" and no "content_scripts".
  • Nothing to grant at install The permissions list is empty, so Chrome has nothing to ask you about when you add it. The two optional entries are asked for only when you press their button inside the app. That isn't a policy we promise to keep — it is the manifest, and the browser enforces it.
  • No content scripts It cannot read, change or even see the pages you visit. The extension exists on its own new tab and nowhere else.
  • Nothing runs in the background There is no service worker to run. Close the tab and the extension is not executing — which is also why a price target can only be checked when you open a tab.
  • Every network request is in two files src/api.js and src/widgets-data.js. (A third, src/i18n.js, reads a language file from inside the extension itself.) The source contains no XMLHttpRequest, no sendBeacon, no eval, no document.cookie, and no analytics SDK of any kind.
  • Requests go out, nothing about you goes with them There is no account, no device id and no telemetry endpoint. A price request contains a coin symbol and a currency code.
