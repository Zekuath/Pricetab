## [Unreleased]

### Added
- **Watching an address tells you what actually went wrong** - every failure used to be the same sentence, telling you to check an address that was often perfectly fine. Paste a Solana or TRON or XRP address and it now says so, and says which chains PriceTab can read. If the balance service is busy - it limits how often it can be asked - it says that too, instead of blaming your address. And a Bitcoin Cash address copied with its "bitcoincash:" prefix, which is how most wallets give it to you, now works: it was being refused as if it were nonsense
- **Eight more tokens found at an Ethereum address** - Pendle, Gnosis, Morpho, Nexo, Coinbase Wrapped Staked ETH, Worldcoin, SPX6900 and Ripple USD. Each contract was asked on-chain what it calls itself before being added, and one of them answers with 8 decimal places rather than the usual 18 - assumed, its balance would have been ten billion times too large. Four tokens added earlier also got their full names, so they can be found by typing the name instead of only the ticker
- **The worst fall, as a widget** - the deepest drop from a high to a later low inside the range you are looking at, for the coin on screen. It costs nothing to have on: it is worked out from the prices already drawn. This is the risk figure rather than an entry point, and that is a finding rather than a preference - when trading rules were tested properly the one thing they reliably did was cut the worst fall, while only half of them beat simply holding. A range that only went up says so instead of printing zero
- **What it costs to move your coins, on the widget panel** - two new cards: Ethereum gas and Bitcoin fees. Each shows the price the network is quoting and, underneath, roughly what that means for an ordinary transfer in your currency and how soon it would go through - because nobody prices a transfer from a gwei figure in their head. Neither asks for a permission or a key: both come from places PriceTab was already reading. The Bitcoin figure assumes an ordinary wallet transaction; yours may be larger, and the widget's description says so
- **A locked call can be taken back, in two clicks** - click a called square once and it asks (`UNLOCK?`), click again and it lets go, with the walls opening as it does. It mirrors the two clicks that locked it. A **settled** call cannot be withdrawn: once it has an answer, removing it would be editing the record rather than correcting a slip. This was already possible from the calls panel's `×` with no confirmation at all - which meant a call was only final if you did not know where the panel was, and the person most likely to have locked the wrong square by accident is the one looking at the chart
- **Choose how a sale is matched to a purchase: FIFO, LIFO or HIFO** - in the portfolio, beside the cost basis report it governs. It decides which purchase the next sale consumes, and which one is assumed gone when you reduce an amount by hand. Sales you have already recorded keep the method they were made with, and the report says which on every line - the purchases a past sale consumed are gone, so nothing can honestly re-decide them afterwards. Which methods your country accepts is not the same everywhere, and PriceTab does not calculate tax
- **Merge a backup instead of replacing everything** - "Merge JSON" beside "Import JSON". A coin you do not already hold is added; a coin you do hold is left exactly as it is, so merging the same file twice changes nothing the second time and no position can be corrupted. It says what it did afterwards
- **VWAP under the price** - the average price actually paid across the window you are looking at, weighted by how much changed hands at each level. It appears when the candle data happens to be loaded and never asks for anything of its own. Unlike most things on a trading screen it makes no claim about the future: it is a price that happened
- **"Has this happened before?" - a base-rate panel, and deliberately not a buy signal** - press B. It reads years of this coin's daily closes and answers one question: how often has it been in this state, and what followed? Every figure carries the number of times it is based on, and where that number is small the panel says so instead of printing a comparison - because "up 90% of the time" across ten occasions is a sample of ten wearing a percentage sign. It does not tell you to buy or sell, and that is a finding rather than a preference: nine textbook rules tested over 21,669 daily closes on eight coins produced nothing that survived correction for multiple testing, and on live data the line usually called "overbought" was followed by a *better* than ordinary month on four coins out of six. Nothing is fetched until you open it
- **A target on your whole portfolio** - the price targets panel can now watch the total of everything you hold, not just one coin. "Tell me when what I hold is worth less than X." It is checked whenever a tab is open; unlike a single coin's target it cannot look backwards through history, because how much you held last week is not something the app knows, and inventing it would mean announcing a crossing that never happened
- **The worst fall, beside your portfolio** - how far the total dropped from a high to a later low inside the range you are looking at. This is the one thing that survived when trading rules were tested properly: they reliably cut the worst fall and only half of them beat simply holding. So it is the risk figure, not an entry point
- **Realized profit for the current year** - shown beside the total realized figure when the two differ. It is the calendar year, and it says so: the tax year ends on a different date in many countries
- **The portfolio shows what it is made of** - a ring beside the total, one arc per holding, biggest first. The colours are the value chart's own, so a coin looks the same in both, and each row in the list below now carries its coin's colour as its share bar - the list is the ring's key, so there is no separate block of colour swatches to read. Hovering or tabbing to an arc names it in the middle. Holdings too small to be their own arc are gathered into a neutral "Other"
- **A news panel, and news worth reading in it** - press N, or the newspaper in the top-right corner. Every headline carries its source and how old it is, newest first across all sources, and you can narrow the list three ways: to the coins you follow or the ones you hold, to particular sources, or by typing. The reason it exists is uncomfortable and worth saying plainly: the feed PriceTab was using carried seven outlets, a third of everything from a single aggregator, and at the time this was built it had published nothing for over four days - with nothing on screen saying so. The panel now shows the age of every source, so a feed that has stopped looks like a feed that has stopped
- **Six real newsrooms, if you want them** - Cointelegraph, Decrypt, CryptoSlate, Bitcoin Magazine, CoinJournal and BBC Business. Browsers do not let a page read another site's feed without permission, so PriceTab asks Chrome for it - from a button in the news panel, never at install, and never until you press it. Nothing is sent to any of them; PriceTab only reads what they publish. One button turns it back off
- **"What happened here?" - the headlines from around a sudden move** - the chart marks the moments the price did something unusual for the range you're looking at, and clicking a mark shows what was being written in the days around it. The archive goes back years, so it works on a spike from 2021 as well as on one from this morning. It costs nothing to have on: where the marks go is worked out from the prices already on your screen, and only opening one asks for anything. What it deliberately doesn't do is tell you why the price moved - headlines from the day of a move are what was being said at the time, and the card says exactly that. Off by default; Settings → Chart → What Happened Here
- **Holder mode now narrows the headline row to what you hold** - it was the one mode that turns the row on and the one mode that never said what the row should carry

### Changed
- **Settings stopped eating the top and bottom lines** - text scrolling into the panel is meant to fade in and out rather than be sliced, and the fade was a quarter of its intended size: 5.5 pixels under a 16-pixel line, which is a slice with a soft edge. A description arriving at the top became an unreadable smear under the tab strip and a switch leaving at the foot was cut through the middle. The panel is also taller now, so there is a quarter more of it on screen and less to scroll past
- **A price service that keeps refusing is left alone for a while** - the tab used to try again every thirty seconds forever, which on a blocked or throttled connection is thousands of requests nobody was going to answer. It now waits longer after each failure, up to five minutes, and goes straight back to normal the moment one request succeeds, you come back to the tab, or you change coin or range. Nothing changes while things are working
- **Watching an Ethereum address is one request instead of two** - the ether balance now comes from the same place as the token balances, in the same call. Beyond being quicker, it keeps Ethereum away from the service that limits how often it can be asked and then refuses everything for a while - which is what made watching several addresses in a row stop working
- **The portfolio no longer opens on "$0.00"** - with nothing tracked, the biggest thing on the screen was a money figure saying your holdings were worth zero, under the words "Total value". It says "Nothing tracked yet" instead. The dashed box that sat above the form has gone too, along with the privacy sentence it repeated from the footer four lines below it, and the whole screen is centred rather than pressed against the top with half a window of black underneath. Import JSON now looks like the button it always was, so all three ways to get data in look like ways in. Separately: if you hold coins but no price has arrived for any of them, that now shows as "—" rather than "$0.00", which was saying something quite different
- **A tab sitting open costs almost nothing now** - the pulsing dot at the live price was animating its radius, which makes the browser redraw the chart on every frame of a loop that never ends. It is drawn as a scale instead, which looks exactly the same and is free. Measured over twenty seconds of an idle tab: 3.1% of a processor core before, 0.1% after - the same as having no animation at all. This is a new tab page, so sitting open is what it does, and the cost was being paid even when the dot was hidden
- **The widget panel scrolls instead of running off the bottom of the screen** - with every card turned on the column was 353 pixels taller than a 1280x800 window, and there was no rule anywhere making it scroll: the last cards simply could not be reached. It now stops at the window and scrolls, with a scrollbar that matches the theme like every other list
- **Widget titles say what they are** - `BTC OPEN INT.` and `BTC LIQS 24H` sat in the same column as `FEAR & GREED`, and the open-interest card was titled with an abbreviation and then printed the full words directly underneath it, which is the abbreviation spending the room it saved. Titles are the full name now, and where a figure belongs to one exchange the card says which
- **Fear & Greed lost its coloured dial** - the needle swung across a red-to-green arc, which is the same claim the RSI widget's "oversold / overbought" labels were removed for a few days earlier: a colour telling you which end is the good end. It is the same plain 0-100 meter the RSI widget uses now, with the number and the source's own word for it
- **Every widget waits the same way** - eleven cards said "Loading..." in the middle of an empty box; they now show the shape of what is coming, which is the difference between a card that is filling in and a card that is broken
- **Picking a coin for a price target is a search box now** - it was a dropdown listing all eighty-one coins, and a dropdown only jumps by first letter, so reaching Synthetix meant scrolling a list as tall as the panel. Type "snx" or "synthetix" and it finds it; your own coins come first. Targets you have already set are untouched
- **Headlines underline when you reach for them** - a headline is a link and now says so on hover and on keyboard focus, rather than either looking like plain text or being permanently underlined. The line fades in and is always reserved, so a list of headlines does not shift as the pointer runs down it
- **Advertising is kept out of the news, and the way it is kept out changed** - press releases and exchange marketing were arriving as if they were reporting. One source was running five advertisements in twenty stories, three of them consecutive posts about the same exchange. Filtering these by their wording does not work: "MEXC's August 2026 Proof-of-Reserves Confirms User Assets Fully Backed" is a headline, and a rule broad enough to catch it starts throwing away real stories, because real stories also say "announces". What works is that the outlets file this material separately themselves - in a press-releases section, or under a byline belonging to the wire service that distributes it. PriceTab now reads that, and for two of the sources it asks the outlet not to send it at all. Anything caught is dropped rather than badged: a "sponsored" label is still the advertisement on your screen
- **Blockchair is no longer one of the news sources** - it needed no permission, which is what kept it there. On the day this was written its newest story was five days old and seven of its ten stories were not in English, from sites that republish rather than report. That is exactly the staleness the news panel was built to show you, shipping as the default. A fresh install now starts with Hacker News alone until you turn the newsrooms on - a thinner panel, and an honest one. Blockchair still supplies the archive behind "what happened here?", where nothing else can reach back years, and it is still one of the sources for watched address balances
- **Scrollbars match the theme everywhere** - the browser draws its own unless it is told not to, and on the dark theme that is a pale grey bar down the side of a black panel. Three lists had already been dealt with and four had not, so half the extension matched itself and half did not. All of them now do, including the new news panel
- **The RSI widget no longer labels its ends "Oversold" and "Overbought"** - those words describe a specific, standard version of RSI, and the number in the widget isn't it: its period follows whatever range you're on, so the same coin at the same moment reads 37.8 on one range and 80.9 on another. The words were also wrong about the standard version - across eleven years of daily prices on eight coins, the thirty days after "overbought" were better than the coin's ordinary month more often than not. The bar now shows the scale it is on, 0 to 100, and leaves the reading to you

### Changed
- **Searching for a coin to add to the portfolio works the way it does everywhere else** - it was a plain substring match that ranked nothing; it now uses the same ranked search as the "/" jumper and the price targets, so typing "eth" puts Ethereum first. It also offers everything the portfolio can actually keep: a few tokens an Ethereum address commonly holds could previously be added only by watching an address, never by typing their name
- **The portfolio's colours are the app's own now** - the six bright colours behind the allocation bar, the holdings rows and the value chart were neither PriceTab's palette nor the coins' own brand colours; they were arbitrary. They are now one green, going from deepest for your largest holding to faintest for your smallest, so the bar reads as an order without looking anything up. Two things had to be got right for green to work: it is the colour red-green colour blindness flattens hardest, so the shades travel from a blue-green to a yellow-green rather than just getting lighter — a difference that kind of vision can still see; and green already means "up" on that screen, two inches from your profit figures, so every shade is kept clearly apart from it. Each coin's name inside the bar switches between black and white to stay readable on its own shade. One honest cost: six unrelated colours were easier to tell apart at a glance if you have normal colour vision - but they were much harder if you do not, and the new set is the same for everybody
- **The allocation ring is a bar now** - the donut has been replaced by a single horizontal strip under the total. Two reasons: the label in the middle of the ring filled the hole from edge to edge and looked cramped, and a circular five-colour object was the only one of its kind in an interface that is otherwise monospaced text in two colours. The strip is the same drawing as the share bar on every row of the list below it, so the header and the table finally match, and each segment wide enough to hold a label now names itself instead of waiting to be hovered
- **The chart behind the portfolio is quieter** - it is meant to be wallpaper and it was not: at its old weight it was the most prominent thing on the screen, with a hard line running straight through the holdings. There is a chart you can actually read one button away
- **A new tab asks for a lot less** - every price series was treated as out of date after thirty seconds, including the year and all-time charts, whose points are a day and a fortnight apart. So each new tab re-fetched six charts for one coin, and so did every coin switch. Each range now goes stale when it could actually have gained a point. Measured on the same profile: a tab opened two minutes after the last one asked for eight things and now asks for three; ten minutes later, nine and now five. Nothing looks different - the chart already painted instantly from the last tab's copy, and the price itself is still fetched fresh every thirty seconds

### Fixed
- **The "Called it" card puts itself away** - it had a × and nothing else, so a call that settled while the tab was in the background left a card over the chart until somebody closed it, which on a new tab page can be days. It now clears itself after about twenty seconds. A hit price target is different and keeps its ×: that is something you asked to be told, and dismissing it is how you acknowledge it. Either way the record is untouched - this closes a card, not an outcome
- **Coming back to a tab you left open refreshes it** - the chart only caught up if a refresh had happened to fire while you were away, and Chrome freezes timers in background tabs, so on the tab this extension actually lives in that often never happened. You would return to the prices from whenever you left until the next refresh came round. It now asks whether what is on screen is stale rather than whether anything was attempted while you were gone
- **The allocation ring named a coin when you were not pointing at anything** - with nothing hovered it fell back to showing the largest holding, exactly as though the pointer were on it. The strip that replaced it says how much of your value sits in one holding, and names a coin only when you reach for one
- **When the price source is blocked, the chart arrives seven times sooner** - some networks and regions sit behind something that refuses the price API outright. PriceTab already switches to a second exchange when that happens, but it spent seven seconds retrying the blocked one first, on a schedule designed for a server that is briefly overloaded. A refusal is not an overloaded server; it answers the same way four seconds later. The retrying now stops after one attempt, and the second exchange is reached in about a second. Measured with the first source refusing everything: a chart on screen at 7.1 seconds before, 1.1 seconds after - and for those seven seconds the tab had been showing the word "PRICE" with nothing under it
- **The portfolio's value chart was mixing up dates between coins** - it drew each holding's history by counting points rather than by reading their dates, and different coins are not quoted at the same rate. On the ALL range this was severe: Bitcoin's whole-history points sit about thirteen days apart and a coin listed in 2023 has its points less than four days apart, so the chart was adding a 2014 Bitcoin price to a 2023 price for the other coin and labelling the result with one coin's dates. The "vs BTC" figure came off the same mistake and was the loudest sign of it - on a portfolio of those two coins it credited Bitcoin with fifteen thousand per cent, its entire life, against the hundred and ninety per cent it actually did over the window on screen. Every holding is now read at the same moments; where one is quoted less often, its last known price is held rather than a price invented between two of them. Portfolios holding Monero, Pi, Mantle and the other coins served from the second exchange were affected on every range, not just ALL
- **A purchase price now remembers which currency you typed it in** - change the display currency and every cost basis was simply re-read in the new one: fifteen thousand dollars became fifteen thousand euros, and the profit on the row, the total at the top, the cost line on the chart and the exported report all agreed with each other and were all wrong. Purchases and sales are stamped with their currency from now on. Anything in a different currency from the one on screen is shown in its own currency and left out of the totals rather than converted, with a note saying so - the same thing a price target set in another currency already does. Converting it at today's rate would give you a profit figure that moves on days you did nothing
- **The total and the percentage beside it now cover the same holdings** - the total counts everything you hold; the change next to it comes from the chart, which draws the twelve largest and cannot draw anything no exchange publishes a price history for. When those differ the screen now says which holdings are on the chart, how much of your value they are, and names the ones it cannot draw
- **Removing a holding, or importing a backup over one, can be undone** - both were a single click with no warning, and removing a holding took its purchases and its recorded sales with it. Those are numbers that exist nowhere but on your own machine. An Undo now sits under the list until you close the portfolio, and it brings back the records, not just the coin
- **A console error on every new tab** - the check for whether Chrome had granted the news permissions read the error only when the answer was yes, which is the one case where there is no error to read. Chrome logged it as unhandled on every load
- **The portfolio no longer reports beating Bitcoin by "−0.0 points"** - a gap of a few hundredths took its sign from the unrounded figure and its digits from the rounded one, so a dead heat was printed as a small loss, in red. Anything that rounds to zero now reads as the dead heat it is
- **Chart Details filled in only after you moved the pointer a second time** - hovering the chart asks for the candles it needs, and nothing redrew the readout when they arrived, so the first hover of a session showed the price and the time and no open/high/low/close at all. It looked like the hour chart in particular had no details, because the hour chart is the one a tab opens on and therefore always the hover that waits for the fetch; switching range and coming back appeared to fix it only because that is another pointer move
- **The switch that turns off the headlines under the price is where you would look for it** - it was third inside a closed section called "Under the price", which is not where anyone looking straight at the headlines thinks to go. That section now opens with the tab and the headline switch is first in it. A stale line in the same panel that credited the headlines to a source no longer used has been corrected
- **Settings text was being cut in half at the top of the list** - scroll the preferences list and a section heading stopped half a line under the tab strip, sliced clean through its letters, so two lines of type met with nothing between them. The list now fades at both edges: content arrives and leaves instead of being cut
- **The "what happened here?" card closes when you click away from it** - it had Escape and its own ×, which are two ways out for someone who already knows they are there. A small card floating over a chart you click for other reasons should get out of the way when the next click plainly is not about it
- **"What happened here?" was returning nothing on any recent chart** - clicking a marked price move said "nothing in the archive for those days" about days that were full of news. The archive it asked had published nothing for five days, so every mark on an hour, day or week chart came back empty while the feature looked broken rather than quiet. It now asks three archives and merges them: the original deep one, Hacker News (which needs no permission, so a fresh install has it, and which reaches back far enough to find the May 2021 crash), and CoinJournal where you have allowed it. Measured on the same windows that used to return nothing: twelve to nineteen stories
- **"Turn on full sources" now visibly works** - the permission was granted and the panel said it was reading six newsrooms, but the list underneath could stay empty. Three separate faults: a refresh asked for the moment permission was granted was dropped if any other news request happened to be in flight, and nothing retried for ten minutes; the panel's "Fetching headlines…" was shown whenever the list was empty rather than while a request was actually running, so a fetch that had finished with nothing looked exactly like one still going, for ever; and the permission was checked for all six sites at once, so revoking one of them from Chrome's settings read as revoking all six. The panel now says how many sources it is reading when that is fewer than all of them, tells you when a fetch has finished with nothing, and names reloading the tab in the one case where that is what fixes it
- **Picking a coin from the search no longer adds a second one** - clicking a suggestion in Settings → Coins added the one you clicked *and* whichever one was top of the list. The buttons in this extension were never marked as ordinary buttons, so the browser treated them as the one that submits the form they sit in, and the form added a coin of its own. Nothing on screen said so, which is why it lasted as long as it did. Every one of the fifty-two buttons in the extension was carrying the same defect, and a new test now fails if any button is added without saying which kind it is
- **A token you hold but cannot chart is no longer thrown away** - a few things an Ethereum address commonly holds (stETH, wBETH, FDUSD, TUSD) can be read and priced but have no price history at any exchange PriceTab uses. They were being found at your address, added to the portfolio, saved — and silently dropped the next time you opened a tab
- **Mantle is priced from an exchange that means Mantle** - the default source answers for MNT with a figure roughly four thousand times too small; it is quoting something else under the same three letters. It now comes from the same exchange the other unlisted coins do
- **Switching coin no longer blanks the screen** - the chart, the price and the range buttons were all replaced by grey placeholders while the new coin loaded, and the chart that came back re-drew itself from the left. The chart now stays where it is, fades while it is out of date, and grows into the new coin's shape when it arrives; only the figures are covered, and nothing on the page moves. Auto-rotate gets the same treatment on every step
- **Nothing on the chart jumps when you switch range or coin any more** - the price line always moved smoothly, but almost nothing else did. The grid and its price and time labels were simply rewritten, so on a chart with the grid or the calls board on — where the lattice is most of what you're looking at — the picture changed between two frames while the line took half a second to follow. The old grid now dissolves into the new one, the call boxes fade in with it, and everything on the chart takes the same six hundred milliseconds: the time a chart takes to draw itself when a tab opens, because it's the same event
- **Switching range no longer skipped the animation outright** - the board's reach is remembered per range, so moving between two ranges you'd zoomed differently looked to the chart like someone reaching for the zoom, and it started a zoom movement. That movement drives the chart frame by frame, which means the new prices were put on screen the instant they arrived rather than travelling there. A range switch isn't a zoom — the board you arrive at belongs to that range — so it no longer pretends to be one
- **The fill no longer comes away from the line during a change** - the shaded area under the price ran its own animation, so partway through every range or coin change it was describing a different price from the line above it — most visibly as a wedge of colour with no line on it. It's now built from the line itself at every frame, so the two cannot disagree, and the change costs less work per frame than the two of them arguing did

### Changed
- **Watching an Ethereum address now finds a lot more of what is in it** - the scan covered 28 tokens and covers 47, including wrapped ether and bitcoin, DAI, UNI, stETH, PayPal USD, PAX Gold, Tether Gold, Ondo, Ethena and others. It costs nothing extra: the balances still come back in a single request, and every one of the additions is already priced by a request the price bar makes anyway. Each contract was asked what it is before being added — one candidate was rejected for calling itself something other than the name it trades under
- **The calls panel's settings were tidied** - three rows of controls that each looked like a different kind of control, with the way *out* of the feature as the loudest button on the screen. Turning calls off is now a button like the others rather than a filled slab; the board's zoom is one stepper with its position in the middle, which is also the way back to the default, so pressing one arrow no longer shifts the other out from under the pointer; and clearing your score — the one thing here that cannot be undone — says so when you reach for it
- **The calls screen, switched off, says what it is keeping** - it was six centred lines of explanation with the one useful fact, that calls you have already made are still being settled, tucked underneath the button. That fact is now at the top with your record beside it, which the switch keeps and the screen never showed

## [1.4.0] - Calls, Portfolio & Settings Polish (packaged 19 Aug 2026, not yet submitted)

### Added
- **One press puts the board's zoom back where it started** - the reach is remembered per range and outlives the tab, so a board pushed several notches out stays out, and clicking back was guesswork because nothing said which notch was the normal one. The number between the − and + is now the way home: press it and the board returns to its default reach. It underlines itself when there's something to undo, and goes back to being a plain label when there isn't. The calls panel has the same reset beside its own − and +
- **The price bar appears about four times faster** - it did its one bulk request, got every coin back, and then still walked all sixty-six in groups of four, redrawing the page after each group and pausing half a second before the next. Measured on the ordinary path: eleven seconds to a finished ticker, now three
- **The chart stops redrawing for things that have nothing to do with it** - the theme object was rebuilt on every update, which every styled element and the chart itself read as a change. A scrolling tab title used to redraw the whole chart; measured at five redraws for five title updates, now none
- **Widget cards arrive without stalling each other** - each of the eight results updated the page separately. They now settle together within a frame, while a slow one still arrives on its own instead of holding up the seven that answered
- **The same data is never fetched twice at once** - opening the portfolio while the price bar was refreshing could send the largest request twice, and two parts of the chart could ask for the same candles simultaneously. Whoever asks second now waits on the first answer
- **Your data survives a full storage quota** - caches are capped by how many entries they hold, not by size, so a browser at its storage limit could silently fail to save a holding you had just typed. A failed save now clears cached prices and headlines to make room and tries again; nothing you entered is ever discarded to make space
- **Jumping to a coin you don't own opens that coin** - pressing `/` and picking one that wasn't on your list added it correctly and then opened whichever coin used to be last. Pick BNB, get BNB
- **A settings dropdown keeps its own keys** - native dropdowns are driven by letters, so pressing `s` in the Number Format menu to reach "Space separated" closed Settings instead. Anything the browser is already spending a keystroke on is left alone now
- **Move headlines work on a fresh tab** - with the headlines setting on and the scrolling news row off, a new tab never loaded any headlines at all: the shared loader only started for the row. Either one starts it now
- **A holding added while prices are refreshing gets a price straight away** - it used to show blank until the next sixty-second refresh, because the request it triggered was dropped for arriving during one already in flight. Refreshes that arrive mid-flight are now honoured when the current one finishes, and a slow refresh can no longer overwrite newer prices with older ones
- **The cached news row is checked before it's trusted** - headlines restored from local storage went to the screen unchecked, so an edited or corrupted cache could put a non-HTTPS link into the row. They now pass the same rules the feeds themselves are held to; a bad link costs the link, not the headline
- **Calls have their own button and their own key** - they were a tab inside the price-targets panel, which meant reaching a result you'd been waiting for went through a panel named after something else. Calls now sit in their own control in the corner, next to the targets bell, and open with `K`. A dot on that button says a call has settled since you last looked — which is the whole reason to come back to it
- **A call is still judged when calls are switched off** - turning the feature off used to freeze every call you'd already made. - **Fixed: a coin could only be removed with a mouse** - the × on a tracked coin's chip was not a button, and the chip it sat in did nothing when pressed. Tab now reaches the ×, Enter removes the coin, and screen readers hear "Remove BTC" instead of nothing
- **Fixed: dragging a coin dropped it one place off** - the list rearranges as you drag, and letting go applied the move a second time, so a coin dragged to the front landed second. It stays where the drag showed it
- **Every dropdown in Settings can be heard** - the seven selects had a visible label beside them but no name of their own

Both are fixed: a hit is announced in the tab title, alternating with a marker while the tab is away so the movement catches your eye the way an unread count does, and settling to a steady line the moment you look at it. Targets are now checked while the tab is hidden as well. This stays a zero-permission extension — writing your own tab's title needs no permission, which is the whole reason the feature is in-tab rather than a browser notification
- **…and you can switch it off** - Settings → Preferences → "Announce Targets In The Tab Title". The switch governs the background checking too, not just the message, because that's the part that costs something: it is the only thing PriceTab fetches while you're looking elsewhere, and it does so only when you have a target armed, only for that target's coins, and every two minutes
- **Fixed: selling reported a gain on coins you no longer had** - there was no way to record a sale, so selling meant editing the amount down by hand — and that left the purchase lots untouched. A holding bought at 30,000 and sold in half still carried the full original cost basis, so a position now worth 45,000 kept reporting the whole position's 60,000 gain. Cost basis is now clamped to what you actually hold, wherever the mismatch came from: a recorded sale, a hand edit, or an import
- **Record a sale, and see what you actually made** - the expanded holding takes "Sold" as well as "Bought" — the same two questions, how much and for how much. Recording one does the three things that have to happen together: takes the coins off, consumes the oldest purchases against them (FIFO), and keeps the resulting gain, which the lots it consumed can no longer be asked for. A **Realized** figure joins the portfolio stats: settled money, sitting beside the unrealized position rather than folded into it. Selling coins you never logged a purchase for is handled honestly — the gain covers the part that had a cost, and the rest is reported as proceeds with nothing to set against them
- **"Tax report" is now "Cost basis report"** - the old name promised a document you could file from, and the file is the record one is worked out *from*: it holds only what you entered in PriceTab, with no exchange history, transfers, fees, crypto-to-crypto trades or staking income. The caveat also moved to where it gets read — on the button, before the click, rather than in a footnote inside a file you've already downloaded — and the file now opens by saying what it is and what it doesn't contain
- **The cost basis report pairs each sale with the purchases it consumed** - a disposal on its own is half a record. Every tax form and every tax tool asks the same thing in the same shape: which acquisition, which disposal, what it cost, what it fetched. A single line per sale can't answer that, because one sale can eat several purchases bought on different days at different prices — and each of those pairs has its own holding period, so the same sale can be part long-term and part short. The Disposals table is now one line per pair, with the acquisition date, the sale date, the days held and the term. Proceeds are split across the pairs by amount, which is arithmetic rather than an allocation: a sale happens at one price, so every unit fetched the same. Lines that have no acquisition to name are still emitted — the uncovered part of a sale, and sales recorded before the pairing existed — because a proceeds column that doesn't add up to what you received is the first thing anyone checks
- **The report has a Disposals section** - it used to state outright that it recorded no sales, which made it half a report. Every recorded sale is now a line with its date, proceeds, the basis it consumed and the realized gain, and the summary block carries the total. The disclaimer that replaced it is the accurate one: sales are in there if you recorded them, and anything sold outside PriceTab is not
- **The portfolio chart says something now** - the full-bleed chart behind the portfolio was texture: no axis, no readout, nothing you could take a number off. It now carries your cost basis as a labelled line across it, so where the curve sits above that line is where you're ahead and where it crosses is when you got there. - **The portfolio stats found a shape** - seven equal-weight figures in one flat run had grown to about 950px in a 760px column, wrapping into two ragged lines of 10px uppercase with nothing to say where to look first. - **Holdings are ordered by what they're worth** - the list rendered in the order coins were added, so your biggest position could sit at the bottom — while the chart behind it was already ranking the same holdings by value to decide which twelve to draw. Biggest first is now the default, with one-tap ordering by value, P/L, today's move or name, remembered between visits. - **Every holding shows its shape** - a small curve on each row, over whatever range the chart is on. - **Portfolio: how you did against just holding BTC** - a portfolio's own percentage answers "did it go up", which in a market that moves together is nearly everyone's answer. It can't be read for the part that was actually yours: whether holding *these* coins beat holding the obvious one. The stats row now carries the gap in percentage points over whichever range the chart is on, with both numbers spelled out on hover. The comparison is exact rather than indicative — the value series holds amounts fixed across the window, so there are no deposits or withdrawals inside it to distort either side, and the benchmark is trimmed to the portfolio's own window so it can't win on span. Dragging is precise but it's a chore past a handful of coins, and these are orders you want back regularly rather than once. The previous order goes into the same undo the reset uses, so a sort can't silently discard an arrangement you dragged into place

Up to 10 targets; ones set in a different display currency show as paused rather than compared against the wrong number
- **Quick coin jumper** - press `/` on the new tab, type a symbol or name and hit Enter. Your own coins rank first; picking a coin you don't track yet adds it and switches to it
- **Retry on failure** - when prices can't be fetched the banner now says so plainly and offers a Retry button instead of silently sitting on cached data
- **Price flash** - the price tints green or red for a moment whenever it moves, so a tab left open visibly reacts (colour only, no layout shift; coin switches don't flash)
- **Since your last visit** - a quiet line under the price shows how the coin moved since you last looked ("Since your last visit (3h ago) +1.24% · $512"), switchable from Settings → Appearance. The reference point is the price you last saw before a break (20+ minutes away), and it holds still for the whole browsing session, so the comparison stays meaningful instead of always measuring against a few minutes ago
- **Chart crosshair with OHLC + volume** - hovering the chart reads out that point's open, high, low, close and volume (from Coinbase candles, fetched only once you actually hover — tabs you just glance at cost nothing extra). Switchable from Settings → Appearance → Chart Details; ranges or currencies without candle data fall back to the price and date (clock times on 1H/1D, plain dates on longer ranges); the readout follows the nearest data point, flips sides at the edges and stays inside the chart. Built to stay cheap: no React re-render per pointer move, a binary search over pre-scaled points, and DOM writes batched into one animation frame
- **One-time rating ask** - after two days of use, a small dismissible card in the corner of the new tab asks for a store rating; it appears exactly once and never returns after dismissing or rating
- **Hacker News in the news ticker** - well-upvoted crypto stories from the past week (via Algolia, CORS-enabled, no key) join Blockchair's headlines
- **News quality filter** - SEO/promo spam ("price prediction", presales, casinos, airdrops…) is dropped and the same story from multiple outlets is collapsed into one headline
- **Portfolio v2: purchase lots** - log purchases the way you made them ("bought 0.5 for 15,000, another 0.2 for 7,000"); each holding keeps a dated lot list that drives cost basis and unrealized P/L (per row and as a headline stat); allocation share per holding; JSON backup/restore (validated against the coin whitelist on import); a cost basis CSV with a per-coin summary plus every dated lot (informational only, not tax advice)
- **Address watching** - paste one of your addresses and PriceTab works out the rest: the address itself says which chain it is on (BTC, ETH, LTC, DOGE, BCH, ZEC), and every positive balance it holds becomes a holding — including, on Ethereum, the 29 supported ERC-20 tokens, found in a single batched call: a coin can combine several watched addresses with a hand-entered amount, and the row expands into a breakdown showing exactly how much came from each source (with each address shown in full and its own "Stop" button); compact ⛓ chips summarise everything being watched, and each address's amount stays synced to its public on-chain balance (read-only lookup via mempool.space / Blockchair, 10-minute cache; the address is stored locally and sent only to the balance provider). For BTC the purchase lots are inferred from the real transfer history — every incoming transfer counts as a buy at that date's estimated price, outgoing transfers consume the oldest lots first; other chains start from one lot priced at the watch date and log later balance increases as new buys
- Viewport meta tag for responsive rendering; Google site verification file (promo site)

- **Far fewer ticker requests** - the price ticker bar, watchlist and top movers now share one bulk market-data request instead of two requests per coin
- Coin prefetching only runs when the browser-tab title ticker or auto-rotate actually needs it

### Internal
- Split the single 7,700-line `app.js` into 12 focused script modules (no build step added)
- Centralized all localStorage access behind shared, validated helpers
- Added a regression test suite (`tests/`) and a CI workflow that runs it on every push
